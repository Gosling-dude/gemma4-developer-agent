# R016-single-agent: the R015 coder as the only agent — evaluated: 9/24 vs R015 10/24, gate FAILED

**Hypothesis (one):** removing the separate localization stage and letting the thinking-enabled coder navigate and
repair the repository directly reduces wasted time and increases correct task resolutions.

**Control:** R015-v13-thinking (10/24 on the 2026-10-09 harness; implementation `d587250`, archive `261c821a…`).
R014's old-harness 13/24 is not a valid comparison: the harness changed.

## Evidence (docs/r015.md)
- With thinking on, R015's read-only localizer used a median ~233 s of the 330 s budget.
- 5/24 R015 sessions never left the localizer (rich_3180, rich_3063, rich_3061, rich_3468, rich_3471).
- 13 of R015's 14 failures hit the 5.5-min cap.
- Public evidence, not reproduced by us:
  - The independently reproduced 0.18 bundle is a single agent.
  - A controlled ablation measured an analyzer stage at +1 (discussion 746250).

## Pre-registered gate (recorded before the R016 run)
Control: R015 = 10/24, 13 time caps, same 24 tasks, harness, notebook and limits.

**Strong success requires all of:**
1. ≥ 14/24 resolved.
2. wins − losses ≥ 3 against R015 (paired, per task).
3. No unhandled tool-contract or architecture failures (harness errors, crashes).
4. Time-cap failures < 13.
5. No substantial increase in incorrect patches or regressions: wrong fix after an edit ≤ R015's count + 2.
6. A meaningful reduction in pre-edit time: median coder seconds to first edit below R015's, and fewer never-edited tasks.

**Not a success:** 13/24 or fewer. Even then, the traces are analysed and the result explained; speed alone is not success.
A local pass does not establish a 0.20 public score. This is a screening gate.

## What the traces show
- **Removing the localizer did remove its up-front cost.** The first edit comes about 2 minutes earlier, and no session is stuck
  in a separate stage. The tasks R015 lost entirely to localization (rich_3180, rich_3063, rich_3061, rich_3468, rich_3471)
  now run the coder from t = 0.
- **The freed time went into the coder's own exploration, not into solves.** It still makes about 20 tool calls before its first
  edit, about as many as R015's two stages together. 12 of 15 failures are still "no edit, time cap".
- At about 8 s per thinking step, the 5.5-min cap allows about 40 steps. The prompt's "first edit by call 12" and half-budget rule are
  not followed: the median is 20 calls before the first edit, the same weakly followed count rule seen in R010 and R014.
- The three R015 no-edit tasks that R016 did edit (rich_3278, rich_3052, rich_3061) all became wrong fixes.
- The one win (rich_3480) came from an edit at 293 s. The two losses (rich_2725, fastapi_11194) are R016 runs that never edited.
  These 1-vs-2 per-task flips are within run-to-run noise (same-build swings of ±2 / 24 seen before).

**Verdict: R016 FAILS its promotion gate (9/24 vs R015's 10/24).** The architectural hypothesis holds at the mechanism level:
the stage cost is gone and edits come earlier. But resolution does not improve, because the single coder spends the recovered time
exploring at thinking speed and still runs out of budget before editing. R016 is not a better submission candidate than R015.

## Remaining failure mode and next experiment (proposed, not run)
**No edit before the 5.5-min cap under thinking: 12/15 R016 failures (13/14 in R015).** Prompt count rules are not followed.
The two remaining levers both concern time:
- the budget the independently reproduced 0.18 bundle uses (8 min / 28 calls / 240 s), which a hard call cap enforces;
- a structural "edit by step N" guard (prompt rules have failed four times: R010, R014, R015, R016).

Any budget change must be checked against the 12 h total (R016: ~304 s/task → ~10.1 h for 120 tasks).
